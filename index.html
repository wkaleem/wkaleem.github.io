About

I am a PhD candidate in Industrial Engineering and Operations Research at Penn State, advised by Anirudh Subramanyam. My research integrates machine learning and high-performance computing with discrete optimization, for decisions in freight and logistics, supply chains, transportation infrastructure and energy systems. Previously, I worked at Argonne National Laboratory and at Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories, where my work on charging schedules is filed as a joint patent.

My research has appeared in Transportation Research Part B and Part C, at the NeurIPS Workshop on Differentiable Learning of Combinatorial Algorithms, and in the IISE Annual Conference Proceedings, where it received the Best Paper Award of the Logistics and Supply Chain Division.

I am an IISE Future Faculty Fellow and an alumnus of the Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing, and was a top-ten finalist for the Lee Schipper Memorial Scholarship.

Research overview

explore research details →

My research centers on three connected thrusts that address the design and operation of freight and energy systems at real-world scale:

  • AI-integrated discrete optimization in freight and logistics. I integrate machine learning (which can learn the operational costs that strategic decisions induce) with classical algorithms for mixed-integer optimization, by representing trained networks exactly within the optimization model.
  • Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty. I model infrastructure and fleet decisions as one sequential decision problem, and design policies that keep each stage an exact mixed-integer program yet approximate only the future, for uncertainties that the decisions themselves influence.
  • Optimizing complex systems on exascale and GPU platforms. I re-design classical algorithms for discrete optimization (decomposition, branch-and-bound, local search) for GPUs and multi-node supercomputers, from a metropolitan-scale model solved across 680 cores to primal heuristics with no CPU counterpart.
