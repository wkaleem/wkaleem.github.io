Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

My work spans charging-network planning, sequential freight market clearing, and fleet charging, combining mixed-integer optimization, queueing theory and approximate dynamic programming.

Selected research

  • Extreme-scale EV charging infrastructure planning for last-mile delivery using high-performance parallel computing

    Transportation Research Part B, 2026.

    Station locations and charger capacities are optimized subject to limits on expected waiting and charging time. Shared use of existing depots and new stations nearly halves costs relative to plans restricting each operator to its own newly built stations.

  • Approximate dynamic programming for matching, scheduling, and pricing in truckload freight markets

    Submitted to the Transportation Research Board Annual Meeting, 2027.

    A sequential freight market-clearing policy combines immediate cost and learned marginal truck values under hours-of-service regulations. A case study synthesized from Argonne’s POLARIS freight model increases freight market surplus by two-thirds over myopic market clearing.

  • Tractable EV Depot Scheduling with Closed-Form Nonlinear Charging Dynamics and Physical Vehicle Movement

    In preparation. With Anirudh Subramanyam and Arvind U. Raghunathan.

    Charging schedules account for power limits, jockey movements and battery charging dynamics. Related work with Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories led to a joint U.S. patent application filed in 2026.

Ongoing work

  • Distributionally robust charging-network planning for long-distance EV travel under decision-dependent demand.
  • Sequential depot charging under uncertain vehicle arrival times and states of charge.
