Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

The challenge: Critical infrastructure is planned for demands unknown in advance, through decisions from the dispatch of a truck within the hour to the siting of stations for decades. Perhaps surprisingly, current methods assume the uncertainties exogenous, although the decisions themselves change them.

My approach: My central premise is to model such a problem once, by state, decision, exogenous information, transition and objective, and to design policies that keep each stage an exact mixed-integer program yet approximate only the future, while ensuring that they are rigorous, scalable and interpretable.

Key contributions:

  • At the strategic level, I designed models for siting charging stations when the queues and link flows they serve depend on the siting decision; I exposit that multi-agency collaboration nearly halves the cost of single-agency plans (Transportation Research Part B 2026).
  • For long-distance trips, my model hedges against all distributions in a Wasserstein ball that depends on the siting decisions, and the reformulation remains a mixed-integer linear program (in preparation).
  • At the operational level, I teamed up with Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories to schedule depot charging across two time scales with the physics of the state of charge; the method is filed as a joint patent (U.S. Patent and Trademark Office, 2026).
  • At the real-time level, I designed a market-clearing policy for truckload spot markets whose value function is learned online from the clearing’s dual solutions; it raises the market surplus by two-thirds over myopic clearing (submitted to TRB 2027).

Impact: Practically, the policies are ones an operator can run: the stage decision is exact, the constraints hold, and the prices are dual solutions of the same program.

Research impact

I see future work along several directions: energy delivery networks and oil and gas fields developed in sequence, and a tractable theory of multi-stage optimization under decision-dependent uncertainty.
