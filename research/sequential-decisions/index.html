Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

Critical energy and transportation infrastructure, freight corridors and terminals, charging networks and depots, is planned for demands that are unknown when it is built, through sequences of decisions that range from the dispatch of a truck within the hour, to the charging of a fleet overnight, to the siting of stations for decades. The decisions themselves change the uncertainties. Where charging stations are built changes which trips need charging and how long vehicles wait, and where a truck is positioned changes which loads it can serve the next day. Current planning methods assume these uncertainties are exogenous, and they come in two kinds: deterministic plans that size infrastructure for a single forecast and are redone when it is wrong, and myopic rules that optimize the current stage and ignore its effect on later stages. Scenario-based methods that hedge against the future multiply the combinatorial model of a stage by the number of scenarios, at the expense of tractability.

My premise is to model such a problem once, by state, decision, exogenous information, transition and objective, so that a deterministic plan, a myopic rule and a scenario-based program become policies compared on the same expected objective. Within that framework I design policies that keep the decision at any stage an exact mixed-integer program and approximate only the future, by value functions learned from the dual solutions of that program or by lookaheads over ambiguity sets that depend on the decision. The policies are rigorous, since operational constraints are never violated, scalable to real-world network, fleet and market sizes, and interpretable, since prices and marginal values are dual solutions of the same program.

Results so far

  • Strategic level: siting stations under congestion. Models for locating charging stations and allocating chargers when the queues and link flows they serve depend on the siting decision. Modeling station-charger pairs as queues and solving exactly with two formulations and cutting planes for the nonlinear waiting-time constraints shows that multi-agency collaboration over combined depot and new-station sites nearly halves the total cost of plans confined to new sites and a single agency. When long-distance trips must charge mid-route, the link flows are uncertain and depend on where new stations are sited; a model that hedges against all distributions in a Wasserstein ball depending on the siting decisions still reformulates as a mixed-integer linear program. Both are lookahead policies, as they optimize the siting decision against an explicit model of the queues and flows it induces.
  • Operational level: charging a fleet at its depot. With Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories, scheduling the charging of a fleet that must depart on time through a hierarchy of transformers, power units and charging guns, under time-varying electricity prices. Discrete vehicle-to-charger assignments couple with continuous power flows across two time scales, and charging power depends on the battery’s state of charge; a piecewise-affine inner approximation of the charging physics and symmetry elimination based on infrastructure homogeneity make the schedule solvable. It meets as many departure deadlines as the infrastructure allows at the lowest electricity cost, and Mitsubishi Electric has filed it as a joint patent with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office. I am now casting the depot as a sequential decision problem in which arrival times and states of charge are exogenous information, with the schedule re-solved as vehicles arrive.
  • Real-time level: clearing a truckload spot market. A market-clearing policy in which a neutral broker matches loads to trucks, builds multi-load schedules that respect the federal hours-of-service regulations, and prices the matches. The value of a truck’s position is learned online from the dual solutions of the clearing, a single mixed-integer program solved exactly by branch-price-and-cut, and the same duals yield an individually rational price for each matched load. The policy raises the market surplus by two-thirds over myopic clearing and cuts the empty miles driven between loads, on a market synthesized from Argonne’s POLARIS agent-based freight model with about twelve thousand loads open per weekly clearing.

Where this is going

On the applied side, the siting models and the multi-period scheduling approaches extend to infrastructure developed in sequence under decision-dependent uncertainty, above all oil and gas field development, where a reservoir is characterized only through the wells drilled into it and rigs must be scheduled across wells before the results are known, and where I have built machine learning models of production and well logs. I am also interested in energy delivery networks, hydrogen corridors for heavy-duty freight, natural gas pipelines, and depots and data centers as large flexible loads, in the resilience of intermodal freight and rail to cascading disruptions, and in public transit under extreme weather, where my proposal to jointly plan transit depots and operations under climate-driven uncertainty in Chicago, Göteborg and Hyderabad was a top-ten finalist for the Lee Schipper Memorial Scholarship.

On the methodological side, I want to optimize against a decision-dependent future while keeping the stage decisions exact, with approaches rooted in mathematical programming rather than in online approximation algorithms: a tractable theory of multi-stage optimization under decision-dependent uncertainty, in which the ambiguity set of a stage depends on earlier decisions, and a clearing policy whose separable value function is replaced by a trained neural network represented exactly within the clearing program, as in my first thrust.
