Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

The challenge: Planning and operating critical transportation infrastructure requires decisions that hedge against uncertainty and adapt as it is revealed over time. In EV charging networks, hydrogen refueling corridors and rail classification yards, uncertainties include flows and induced demand, vehicle arrivals and service times, and train arrivals and processing times. Investments can also influence future demand, creating decision-dependent uncertainty.

My approach: Building on Powell’s unified framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, I design mixed-integer optimization policies that minimize immediate cost together with a tractable approximation of future value. My aim is to provide feasibility guarantees under capacity and service constraints at each step, scale to real-world transportation networks, and use dual information from relaxations or decompositions to value scarce capacity.

Key contributions:

  • At the strategic level, I designed charging-network models that determine station locations and charger capacities subject to limits on expected waiting and charging time. Each station and charger type is modeled as a multiserver queue. Combining existing depots and newly built stations for shared use by fleet operators nearly halves the cost of building and using the network relative to plans that restrict each operator to the newly built stations it owns (Transportation Research Part B 2026). In ongoing work, we are extending these models to long-distance EV travel under decision-dependent demand, with an exact mixed-integer linear reformulation for a decision-dependent ℓ1-Wasserstein ambiguity set.
  • At the tactical level, I designed a sequential freight market-clearing policy that matches loads to trucks in multi-load schedules under federal hours-of-service regulations. The policy combines immediate cost with learned marginal truck values that depend on positions and availability. I implemented a specialized branch-price-and-cut algorithm to solve each weekly market clearing exactly. In a case study on a market synthesized from Argonne’s POLARIS freight model, the approach increases the freight market surplus by two-thirds over myopic market clearing (submitted to TRB 2027).
  • At the operational level, I worked with Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories on depot charging schedules for heterogeneous fleets under power limits, jockey movement constraints and battery charging-power limits. The model produces executable schedules that prioritize charging deadlines, delays and electricity cost. Mitsubishi Electric filed a joint patent application with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office in 2026. In ongoing work, we are extending the model to sequential charging decisions under uncertain arrival times and states of charge.

Impact: This work addresses uncertainty at three levels: strategic infrastructure planning, tactical freight market clearing and operational charging of heterogeneous fleets. The existing planning models and sequential freight market-clearing policy motivate extensions that account for both future decisions and decision-dependent uncertainty.

Future directions

I seek to reduce operating costs and service delays with decisions that account for future demand. For freight market clearing, I will approximate the cost of serving future loads for the fleet as a whole with neural models (Thrust 1), accounting for competition among trucks for the same future loads while retaining separate scheduling subproblems for each truck. I will also extend our static EV charging-network planning model to sequential decision-making, developing neural decision rules for capacity expansion under decision-dependent uncertainty in demand.

From an applied view, I intend to model the phased expansion of EV charging networks and hydrogen refueling corridors, determining when, where and how much capacity to install as demand responds to earlier investments, subject to limits on expected waiting times. In rail classification yards, I will adapt my scheduling approaches to assign tracks and sequence car movements as freight trains arrive, accounting for uncertain arrivals, processing times and capacity needed by later trains to reduce missed outbound connections.
