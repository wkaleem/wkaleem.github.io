Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

The challenge: Critical infrastructure is planned for demands unknown in advance, through decisions from a truck’s dispatch within the hour to a station’s siting for decades, and the decisions themselves change uncertainties that current methods assume exogenous.

My approach: I model such a problem once, by state, decision, exogenous information, transition and objective, and design policies that keep each stage an exact mixed-integer program yet approximate only the future, by value functions learned from dual solutions or by lookaheads over decision-dependent ambiguity sets.

Key contributions:

  • Siting charging stations under congestion, with queues and link flows that depend on the siting decision; multi-agency collaboration nearly halves the cost of single-agency plans (Transportation Research Part B 2026).
  • Distributionally robust siting for long-distance travel, with a Wasserstein ambiguity set that depends on the siting decisions and a reformulation that stays a mixed-integer linear program (in preparation).
  • Depot charging schedules across two time scales, with the physics of the state of charge inside the program; filed as a joint patent with Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories (U.S. Patent and Trademark Office, 2026).
  • A market-clearing policy for truckload spot markets whose value function is learned online from the clearing’s dual solutions, raising the market surplus by two-thirds over myopic clearing (submitted to TRB 2027).

Impact: Policies an operator can run as they are: the stage decision is exact, the constraints hold, and the prices are dual solutions of the same program.
