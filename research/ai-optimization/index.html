AI-integrated discrete optimization in freight and logistics

Freight and logistics networks are designed and operated through decisions at multiple scales: siting depots and terminals for decades, sizing fleets for months, routing vehicles and assigning orders within the day. These decisions interact. The longer-term ones constrain the daily ones, and the cost of a longer-term decision can only be evaluated by optimizing the operations it induces. The cost of opening a depot is the cost of optimally routing the customers assigned to it. Yet the decisions at one scale are usually made with the others fixed or approximated: depots sited by straight-line distance, fleets sized with closed-form routing formulas, orders assigned as if drivers followed the planned routes.

Exact methods cannot close that gap at real sizes, since every candidate design induces an NP-hard routing problem and there are astronomically many designs. My approach integrates machine learning, which can learn the relationship between a strategic decision and the operational cost it induces, with classical algorithms for mixed-integer optimization. A trained network is represented exactly inside the mixed-integer program by additional variables and constraints, and the program is then solved exactly over the learned model.

Results so far

  • Location-routing with neural surrogates. Using the permutation invariance of routing costs and mixed-integer reformulations of ReLU networks, we built a neural surrogate of routing cost that is represented exactly within the location-allocation model. On four benchmark sets the method is accurate, within several percent of the best known solutions and closer on larger instances, fast, more than an order of magnitude faster than existing heuristics on the largest instances, and modular, since one trained model serves any number of customers and depots and any mixed-integer solver. The first paper received the Best Paper Award of the IISE Logistics and Supply Chain Division. With an undergraduate researcher, I am transferring the same surrogate, without retraining, to multi-depot, two-echelon and periodic routing.
  • Unified estimation of routing costs. Many planning decisions need routing costs but not routes. Representing an instance as a collection of sets of customers, vehicles and depots, and showing that the optimal cost is permutation invariant and admits a sum decomposition, one estimator handles five routing variants, with capacities, time windows, heterogeneous fleets and multiple depots, and yields out-of-sample errors two- to fifteen-fold smaller than the best closed-form approximations.
  • Data-driven optimization over realized costs. Drivers do not follow planned routes. A method that learns the realized cost from operational history and embeds it in the order assignment model cuts late deliveries threefold relative to routing-based planning, on two months of public food delivery data from Shanghai, where one in five orders arrives late.

Where this is going

On the methodological side, I want surrogates that yield provable lower bounds on the optimal cost of the original problem, since the predictions of a trained network carry none. Optimizing over trained ReLU networks induces either mixed-integer reformulations that grow with the size of the network or complementarity-based nonlinear reformulations that remain tractable only when all decisions are continuous, and transformers, which give my most accurate surrogates, admit neither. That calls for penalty and decomposition methods for neural network constraints, which I am developing with collaborators in nonlinear and combinatorial optimization. I also want surrogates that are conservative where data is sparse and that are retrained on the routes driven, and I would like to grow the unified estimator into a foundation model of routing cost, pretrained across variants and geographies and adapted to the data of a single carrier.

On the applied side, the same models extend to network design in freight and passenger transportation, such as transfer-hub networks for autonomous trucking and last-mile networks served by trucks and drones together, building on exact routing of heterogeneous fleets of drones and ground vehicles; to production planning integrated with the sequencing of changeovers on the plant floor, where I have started on lot-sizing with data from a food manufacturer; and to oil and gas production, where I have built machine learning models of multiphase flow through surface chokes.
