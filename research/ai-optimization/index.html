AI-integrated discrete optimization in transportation and logistics

The challenge: Transportation and logistics networks are planned at multiple time scales, from the strategic siting of facilities to the operational routing and assignment of orders. These decisions interact. The routes and their cost depend on where facilities are sited and how fleets are sized. Ignoring this interaction can produce plans that appear economical but prove costly to execute.

My approach: The induced operational cost can be computationally expensive to evaluate, available only through a simulator, or observable only through historical data. I integrate machine learning with classical algorithms for mixed-integer optimization to learn surrogates of these costs and represent the trained networks exactly within higher-level planning models.

Key contributions:

  • Neural embedded optimization for location-routing: I designed a permutation-invariant neural architecture and embedded the trained network exactly within the location-allocation model. Across four benchmark sets, the method produces solutions within several percent of the best known solutions and is more than an order of magnitude faster than existing heuristics on large instances. The same trained network handles any number of customers and depots (IISE 2024, Best Paper Award, NeurIPS DLCA Workshop 2025, and under review at Transportation Research Part E).
  • Unified neural estimators of routing costs: I developed a multi-set representation of customers, vehicles and depots. Experiments on five routing variants show errors two- to fifteen-fold smaller than those of the best baselines (submitted).
  • Data-driven on-time last-mile delivery: I learned the total time drivers took to deliver a batch of orders and embedded this estimate within an order assignment model. Experiments on two months of food delivery data from Shanghai reduce late deliveries threefold relative to a routing-based baseline (MIP Workshop 2026).
  • In ongoing collaborative work, I am extending neural embedded optimization to a family of algorithms for multi-depot, two-echelon and periodic routing (working paper).

Impact: These models bring operational costs into planning decisions and support fast what-if analyses in logistics, including fleet sizing and service-zone design.

Future directions

I envision three methodological directions: retraining surrogates with the cost of the decisions they induce, penalty alternating direction methods for neural network embeddings without additional binary variables, and provable lower and upper bounds on the optimal cost of the original planning problem. I also plan to investigate embeddings of trained diffusion and language models.

From an applied viewpoint, I seek to reduce costs and improve productivity through transfer-hub network design for autonomous trucking and joint production and distribution planning. Beyond transportation and logistics, I plan to extend the same AI-integrated approach to machining parameters and drilling decisions.
