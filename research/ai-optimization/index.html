AI-integrated discrete optimization in freight and logistics

The challenge: Freight and logistics networks are designed and operated through decisions at multiple scales, from network-level siting for decades to vehicle-level routing within the day. These decisions interact, and the cost of a longer-term decision can only be evaluated by optimizing the operations it induces. Perhaps surprisingly, the decisions at one scale are often made with those at the other scales fixed or approximated.

My approach: Existing approaches are fundamentally challenged by such multi-scale interactions, since evaluating even one candidate design requires solving an NP-hard problem, across an astronomical number of designs. I integrate machine learning, which can learn the relationship between strategic decisions and the operational costs they induce, with classical algorithms for mixed-integer optimization, to enable the learning and subsequent exact optimization over learned models of those costs.

Key contributions:

  • Location-routing with neural surrogates: a neural surrogate of routing cost represented exactly within the location-allocation model by additional variables and constraints, accurate, fast and modular across four benchmark sets (IISE 2024, Best Paper Award, NeurIPS DLCA Workshop 2025, and under review at Transportation Research Part E).
  • Unified estimation of routing costs: one estimator across five routing variants, with out-of-sample errors two- to fifteen-fold smaller than the best closed-form approximations (submitted).
  • Data-driven optimization over realized costs: order assignment optimized over realized rather than planned travel times, cutting late deliveries threefold on public data (MIP Workshop 2026).
  • The same surrogate transferred, without retraining, to further routing variants, with an undergraduate advisee (working paper).

Impact: Strategic decisions can be optimized over learned operational costs at sizes that exact methods cannot address, with the same trained model serving any instance and any mixed-integer solver.

Research impact

This thrust offers opportunities to advance optimization and learning theory, and applications: surrogates with provable lower bounds on the optimal cost, penalty and decomposition methods for neural network constraints, and the extension of the same models from network design to production planning and oil and gas production.
