Research

AI-integrated discrete optimization in transportation and logistics

Trucks carry more than 12.8 billion tons of U.S. freight a year over networks planned at multiple time scales, from the strategic siting of facilities that last for decades, through the tactical sizing of fleets for months, to the operational routing and assignment of orders within the day. Yet decisions at these scales interact. The operational routes and their cost are a function of where facilities are sited and how fleets are sized. Ignoring this interaction can produce infrastructure decisions that appear economical at first but prove costly in the long term. For example, depots are sited assuming each customer is served from the nearest one, fleets are often sized using static routing-cost formulas, and orders are assigned to drivers assuming they follow prescribed routes (although drivers often deviate from them).

This research pillar focuses on training machine learning models to represent operational costs and embedding them within planning problems. Advances in the mathematical representation of trained neural networks allow these problems to be optimized using off-the-shelf solvers and specialized algorithms. This growing area of research has applications in complex engineering systems such as transportation, supply chains, power systems and oil and gas.

Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

The goal of this pillar is to optimize the planning and operation of critical transportation infrastructure, including EV charging networks, hydrogen refueling corridors and rail classification yards. The decisions must hedge against uncertainty and adapt as new information becomes available. Relevant uncertainties include network-level link flows and induced demand, vehicle arrivals and charging or refueling times at stations, and train arrivals and processing times in rail yards.

Extreme-scale optimization on next-generation computing systems

Advances in CPUs, GPUs and AI accelerators provide an opportunity to rethink optimization algorithms for extreme-scale problems. The aim is to develop hardware-aware methods that use the computing resources powering modern AI to solve large-scale optimization problems. These methods will balance workloads within memory and communication limits to obtain better solutions within a fixed computing budget.
