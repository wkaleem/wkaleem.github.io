Research

Discrete optimization that learns from data and runs at the scale of real systems.

My research aims at improved designs, efficient operations and better decisions across seemingly different but mathematically similar engineering systems: freight and logistics, supply chains, transportation infrastructure, energy and manufacturing. It draws on systems modeling, computational nonlinear and discrete optimization, machine learning, dynamic programming, and high-performance and GPU computing. Each thrust below poses open questions in applied mathematics, new use cases in engineering, management and public policy, and practical models, algorithms and open-source software.

Key research directions

AI-integrated discrete optimization in freight and logistics

Neural surrogates · Exact embedding · Location-routing Trained networks represented exactly inside mixed-integer programs, so that siting, fleet and assignment decisions are optimized over learned operational costs. papers →

Sequential decisions in critical infrastructure under uncertainty

Decision-dependent uncertainty · Value functions from duals · Charging and freight markets Policies that keep each stage an exact program and approximate only the future: rigorous, scalable and interpretable. papers →

Optimizing complex systems on exascale and GPU platforms

Parallel decomposition · GPU-native search · Open-source solvers Classical algorithms re-designed for supercomputers and GPUs, from decomposition across 680 cores to primal heuristics with no CPU counterpart. papers →
