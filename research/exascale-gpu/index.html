Optimizing complex systems on exascale and GPU platforms

The challenge: GPUs and supercomputers can take on problems that were out of reach, but the standard algorithms of discrete optimization were written for CPUs. A siting model for a whole metropolitan area cannot even be loaded into a solver’s memory.

My approach: I rewrite those algorithms for the new machines. Decomposition runs across the partitions of a supercomputer, and search runs on a GPU as a method built for it rather than a port from a CPU.

Key contributions:

  • A decomposition across 680 CPU cores on partitions that cannot talk to one another. It solved a Chicago model with nearly half a million households and two thousand candidate stations, a size no one had solved before (Transportation Research Part B 2026).
  • Primal heuristics that only make sense on a GPU, with a collaborator at the University of Florida. They find feasible solutions on as many benchmark instances as an open-source solver (working paper).
  • A GPU neighborhood search for railcar blocking that evaluates hundreds of thousands of candidate moves in the time it used to take to evaluate one (ongoing).
  • Accepted contributions to NVIDIA’s cuOpt.jl and to VRPSolverEasy.

Impact: Models that no solver can load are solved on the machines the DOE already runs.

Selected publications

  • Extreme-scale EV charging infrastructure planning for last-mile delivery using high-performance parallel computing. with T. Cokyasar, J. Larson, O. Verbas, T. H. Bhuiyan, A. Subramanyam. Transportation Research Part B, 2026 [doi].
  • Contributor, with accepted pull requests, to cuOpt.jl, a Julia interface to the NVIDIA cuOpt solver, and VRPSolverEasy, a branch-cut-and-price exact solver for vehicle routing problems.
