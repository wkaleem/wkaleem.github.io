Optimizing complex systems on exascale and GPU platforms

The challenge: Advances in extreme-scale computing, GPUs and AI accelerators such as TPUs and Cerebras wafer-scale engines offer a unique opportunity to address previously inconceivable optimization problems and applications. This requires rethinking and redesigning classical algorithms for discrete optimization, such as branch-and-bound, to address sequential bottlenecks and exploit the parallelism of these platforms.

My approach: My aim is to solve discrete optimization models at the extreme scale and detail of real engineering systems, while preserving feasibility guarantees and valid bounds on solution quality. This work draws on my training through the NERSC HPC Crash Course at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and the Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing, supported by the U.S. Department of Energy.

Key contributions:

  • Extreme-scale network design in last-mile delivery: I developed a charging-network model with billions of variables and constraints at metropolitan scale. Lagrangian dual decomposition splits the model into station-level subproblems, solved with cutting planes on 680 CPU cores across heterogeneous partitions. Specialized rounding heuristics produced feasible plans for nearly half a million households and two thousand candidate stations in Chicago within six hours, a scale not previously addressed for this charging location-allocation problem (Transportation Research Part B 2026).
  • GPU-native primal heuristics: in ongoing collaborative work, we are developing GPU-accelerated methods to find and improve feasible solutions in mixed-integer optimization. This work grew out of the 2026 Land–Doig MIP Competition, supported by NVIDIA and the Mixed Integer Programming Society. I am the lead developer, implementing GPU kernels in Julia using CUDA.jl.
  • Neighborhood search for railroad blocking: I am experimenting with a GPU-accelerated very large-scale neighborhood search heuristic built on classical neighborhood search. I am the lead developer, implementing GPU kernels in C++ using Kokkos (ongoing).
  • Accepted pull requests to cuOpt.jl, a Julia interface to the NVIDIA cuOpt solver, and VRPSolverEasy, an exact branch-cut-and-price solver for vehicle routing.

Impact: The charging-network model has been used in POLARIS analyses of Chicago’s 2050 policy scenarios, intended to inform the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning’s regional transportation plan and ComEd’s grid investments.

Future directions

I will build on my decomposition approach to obtain better network designs on heterogeneous computing systems within a fixed computing budget. I will develop methods to assign and schedule the resulting subproblems across CPUs, GPUs and AI accelerators, balancing workloads subject to memory and communication limits.

I also plan to solve neural embedded mixed-integer models directly on GPUs and AI accelerators (Thrust 1), keeping trained networks and optimization computations on the same device to reduce data transfers and support repeated solves during retraining. A further direction is mixed-precision algorithms for general mixed-integer programming, motivated by the performance advantage of low-precision matrix arithmetic on AI accelerators.
