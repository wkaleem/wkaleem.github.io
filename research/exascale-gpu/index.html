Optimizing complex systems on exascale and GPU platforms

The challenge: The growth of powerful computing paradigms, including GPUs, AI accelerators and heterogeneous multi-node supercomputers, is a unique opportunity to tackle previously inconceivable problems. But it requires re-designing classical algorithms for discrete optimization, as branch-and-bound, local search and decomposition were all engineered for CPUs.

My approach: I re-design these algorithms so that they effectively exploit modern platforms: decomposition that runs across the partitions of a supercomputer that cannot communicate with one another, and search that is native to the GPU rather than a port of a CPU method.

Key contributions:

  • Facility location under congestion at metropolitan scale: a Lagrangian dual decomposition that exploits parallelism across 680 CPU cores on heterogeneous partitions, solving an instance with nearly half a million households and two thousand candidate stations, a size never addressed in the literature (Transportation Research Part B 2026).
  • Primal heuristics native to the GPU, with no meaningful CPU counterpart, that find feasible solutions on as many competition instances as an open-source solver (working paper, with the University of Florida).
  • Railcar blocking by GPU-accelerated neighborhood search, evaluating hundreds of thousands of candidate moves in the time of one conventional evaluation (ongoing).
  • Accepted contributions to NVIDIA’s cuOpt.jl and to VRPSolverEasy.

Impact: Models that cannot even be loaded in the memory of a solver are solved on the leadership-class machines the DOE already operates.

Selected publications

  • Extreme-scale EV charging infrastructure planning for last-mile delivery using high-performance parallel computing. with T. Cokyasar, J. Larson, O. Verbas, T. H. Bhuiyan, A. Subramanyam. Transportation Research Part B, 2026 [doi].
  • Contributor, with accepted pull requests, to cuOpt.jl, a Julia interface to the NVIDIA cuOpt solver, and VRPSolverEasy, a branch-cut-and-price exact solver for vehicle routing problems.

Research impact

In the future, I see at least three more promising directions: a GPU-native solver for vehicle routing released as open-source software, the freight infrastructure of a nation planned in one model on leadership-class supercomputers, and optimizing over learned models and integer decisions end to end on a single device.
